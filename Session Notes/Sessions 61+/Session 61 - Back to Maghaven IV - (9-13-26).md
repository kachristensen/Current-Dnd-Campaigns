## Summary


## IC Synopsis - Rem's Register
> We continued our travels south towards Lord Aaron’s camp with Captain Harold in tow.  Ulrich and I conversed along the way, but save for a failed attempt to befriend Harold by Lewis it was otherwise quiet.  Upon arrival at the camp, we were told Lord Aaron was ailing and not receiving visitors.  I assured the guard we would be an exception, and my words were proven true in a matter of minutes.  After introducing Lord Aaron to Perse and Valindra, whom he had not met during our last visit, Perse and I informed him of the plague’s origin and purpose, the fruit, and the means to cure the disease.  When we informed him our intention to depose the Rexum, however, Lord Aaron tightened up.  Ulrich tried to assuage him, but the Lord asked for the night to consider his actions.  He had sworn to serve the Rexum, and did not take his vow lightly.  Far be it from me to question a cathar’s resolve, even a former one.  We acquiesced and left Lord Aaron with Ulrich for the evening, while Perse found her instrument and libations to accompany the music.
   Ria was accosted by an admiring spellcaster as she made her way through the camp, but in her typical fashion she avoided answering any of her admiring fan’s questions.  Instead she offered tales of our dragon conquests, which seemed to appease her enthusiastic supporter.  Valindra drew an entirely different sort of attention, though Lewis and Ria did their best to shield her from the racial prejudice.  Valindra asked Lewis for assistance in procuring a bow to replace her crossbow, and he obliged her.  The two found Lord Aaron’s Dread Knight, who served as the camp’s quartermaster among other roles.  He made no effort to hide his disdain for our resident elf, but Lewis was able to quash the worst of it and get her a bow.  Should we take the throne, we can end the enslavement of elves easily enough.  But how do we rid man of the hatred in his heart?
   I had ample time to consider such quandaries as I brought food and ale to Captain Harold.  The two of us ate in silence until Ulrich found me some time later, and informed me that Lord Aaron had requested my presence.  I left Harold with Ulrich and went to see the Lord, who asked me to join him on a walk.  The tension from earlier was gone as Lord Aaron informed me that he did not wish to continue the fight.  I offered to rid him of the plague and see him into retirement, but the former cathar had something else in mind.  Lord Aaron was troubled by his past and the things he’d done in service to the Rexum, specifically the purge of Shadeleaf.  He asked me for the mercy of death, and though it pained me to do so, I agreed.  I felt a failure then, despite Lord Aaron’s assurances that I had given him hope.  I thought then of my father, my brothers, Sally, and my mother.  I remembered our final conversation and the promises made.  I found strength in that conviction to carry on, as I had every single day since she died.  Lord Aaron did not seem so heavy by comparison, as I brought his body back to camp.
   Ulrich greeted me in Lord Aaron’s tent, and assured me he’d see to the transfer of power and assume leadership of Lord Aaron’s forces.  He saw the rawness in my eyes and told me he was proud of me, and that I was growing into a fine leader.  The tears came harder then, and I pulled him into a hug.  I cannot say why I chose that moment, but I asked Ulrich something that had long lingered in my mind.  How many others were made like me, using the sap of Karz’s tree?  To his credit, Ulrich did not shy away from the truth, and told me that despite dozens of other attempts only my treatment was successful.  In my sorrow I attributed this to my susceptibility to illness, but Ulrich took it to mean that I was destined for something greater.  His words were a comfort, but I needed time to process and rest, so I hugged him once more and departed.  I informed Lewis that he could cease his preparations for Lord Aaron’s procedure before retiring for the night. 
   Ria floated a cup of tea to my tent as Lewis magically sent word to Sabine, confirming she still lived and learning the location of her prison.  She warned us to expect horror inside the city, but offered little in the way of specifics.  In the morning, Ulrich gathered the troops to inform them of Lord Aaron’s passing and his assumption of power.  There were questions and murmurs but nothing that gave me cause for concern, as Perse gathered our group and Ulrich together for a conference.  We agreed to enter Maghaven via the cave Harold told us about, and to use the sewers to avoid as much of the horror on the surface as possible.  Where we continued to differ was on the topic of surrender and mercy, with Perse and I arguing once more.  Lewis attempted to mediate, though I was in no mood for compromise and cut him short.  Neither did we reach any substantial agreement, and I left Perse with a threat of trouble should she murder another surrendered foe.
   Lewis and I spoke with Harold then, and learned of two paths through the cave.  The upper path was inhabited by monsters, while the lower path was occupied by local forces.  To avoid any further moral conundrums, we opted for the upper path.  Before we left camp, however, Lewis summoned a Heroes’ Feast and imbued us with divine protection.  As we approached the cave, Valindra shrouded us in magic.  The river was rife with the scent of sewage, but we pressed on until Valindra spotted a troll.  While it was preoccupied with a meal we ventured South, but doubled back at the site of torches to avoid any conflict with the Maghaven forces.  The troll spotted us this time, and as it bellowed out a roar two more trolls emerged.  Two of the three were covered in poisonous pustules, while the third seemed to be rotting from within.  We quickly dispatched the rotting troll, but the poisonous ones expelled their toxins when we wailed on them.  Perse commanded them around, but there was little we could do to avoid the venomous backlash.  Thankfully, Lewis’s feast protected us from the worst of it and we felled the remaining trolls.  We washed the poison off in the river and deliberated our path forward: either following the river or into the troll’s cavern.
